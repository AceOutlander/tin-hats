# Historical research for the campaigns

The facts behind `STALINGRAD.md`, `DDAY.md`, `BERLIN.md` and `AFRICA.md`, with sources, the equipment-by-date tables
used by the era test (`FOUNDATIONS.md` 5.2), and an honest list of where sources disagree.

Method: each claim was checked against at least two sources where possible, usually Wikipedia plus a specialist or
official source. Claims with only one source, or none, are marked:
- **[single]**: only one source found;
- **[unverified]**: no source found; a reasonable reading;
- **[approx]**: a map pin placed by the researcher from the town or feature (accurate to about 1 to 2 km).

Standard reference facts not re-fetched for this report are marked **[ref]**.

Research ran 2026-11-08. One source domain was found unusable: stalingrad.net now redirects elsewhere, and
comandosupremo.com is parked.

## 1. Stalingrad campaign (June 1941 to February 1943)

### Brest Fortress, 22 June 1941
- **Assault**: began about 04:15 Moscow time by the 45th Infantry Division (Generalmajor Fritz Schlieper, about 17,000
  men, 4th Army).
- **German support**: no tanks of its own; a StuG III battery from the 34th Division; 60 cm Karl mortars of Heavy
  Artillery Battalion 833; Nebelwerfers; flamethrowers.
- **Defenders**: about 9,000, from the 6th and 42nd Rifle Divisions and NKVD border guards (Zubachev, Fomin, Gavrilov).
- **End**: the East Fort surrendered on 29 June after Ju 88 bombing; the 45th Division left on 2 July.
- **Losses**: German 429 dead; Soviet more than 2,000 dead and about 6,800 captured.
- **Myths**: the "32 days" story grew from the 1950s. The wall inscription dated 20 July was found after the war; brief
  it as tradition.
- Sources:
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defense_of_Brest_Fortress
  - https://warfarehistorynetwork.com/?p=103751
  - https://www.belarus.by/en/press-center/photo?tid=2051
- Pin 52.083, 23.656 [approx].

### Dubno–Lutsk–Brody, 23 to 30 June 1941
- **Soviet command**: the Southwestern Front (Kirponos; Zhukov at the front HQ). Mechanised corps: 8th (Ryabyshev;
  Popel's group around the 34th Tank Division), 9th (Rokossovsky), 15th (Karpezo, wounded), 19th (Feklenko), 22nd
  (Kondrusev, killed 24 June).
- **German forces**: Panzer Group 1 (Kleist), with the 11th, 13th, 14th and 16th Panzer Divisions.
- **Popel**: attacked at 14:00 on 27 June and took Dubno, then was encircled.
- **Rokossovsky**: refused a renewed attack order; Ryabyshev obeyed it.
- **Tank numbers disagree**, even within Wikipedia: about 750 German against 2,500 to 3,500 Soviet.
- **German Panzer IIIs**: carried both 3.7 cm and 5 cm guns (the 11th Panzer had about 24 and 47 respectively)
  [single: panzerworld table].
- **88s against KVs**: used [ref]. Day-by-day weather: [unverified].
- Sources:
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Brody_(1941)
  - https://insidegmt.com/dubno-41-the-little-known-battle-of-dubno
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/8th_Mechanized_Corps_(Soviet_Union)
  - https://mail.panzerworld.com/barbarossa-1941
- Pin 50.42, 25.73 [approx].

### Volokolamsk Highway, October to November 1941
- **The 316th Rifle Division** (Panfilov): formed in Alma-Ata, about 90% Kazakh and Kyrgyz; arrived on 11 October and
  held 41 km. Volokolamsk fell on 27 or 28 October.
- **Dubosekovo, 16 November**: the 2nd Panzer Division against the 1075th Regiment.
- **Panfilov**: killed on 18 November; the division became the 8th Guards.
- **The "28 Panfilov men"**: a myth, per the 1948 prosecutor's inquiry. The battle itself is real.
- **PTRD**: adopted 29 August 1941; first issued to the 316th before 16 November [single: National Interest; consistent
  with Wikipedia].
- **Katukov**: Mtsensk ambush in early October; 1st Guards Tank Brigade from 11 November.
- **Weather**: first snow 7 October; ground frozen by about 15 November.
- Sources:
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/316th_Rifle_Division
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/8th_Guards_Rifle_Division
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panfilov%27s_Twenty-Eight_Guardsmen
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikhail_Katukov
  - https://nationalinterest.org/node/118526
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Moscow
- Pin (Dubosekovo) 55.98, 36.07 [approx].

### The Moscow counter-offensive, December 1941
- **Start**: the Kalinin Front on 5 December, the Western Front on 6 December.
- **Liberation dates**: Solnechnogorsk 12 December; Klin encircled 13, freed 15 December (371st and 365th Rifle
  Divisions); Kalinin 16 December.
- **Temperatures**: official lows about -28.8 °C, with unofficial readings far lower.
- **German losses to cold**: more than 130,000 frostbite cases.
- **"Siberian divisions"**: overstated. Some Far Eastern divisions came, but most December forces were new reserves.
- Sources:
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Moscow
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/371st_Rifle_Division
  - https://www.realclearhistory.com/2017/06/22/myth_of_stalin039s_039siberian_divisions039_2685.html
  - https://www.operationbarbarossa.net/the-siberian-divisions-and-the-battle-for-moscow-in-1941-42/
- Pin (Klin) 56.33, 36.73 [approx].

### Crossing the Volga, 14 to 15 September 1942
- **13th Guards** (Rodimtsev): about 10,000 men, about 1,000 without rifles. The vanguard was 1st Battalion, 42nd
  Guards Regiment; it lost 400 men on 15 September.
- **Opposition**: the German 71st Infantry Division.
- **The station**: Dragan's group fought there for nearly three weeks. "Changed hands 15 times" is [unverified].
- **The Volga's width**: commonly 1 to 2 km [unverified].
- Sources:
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/13th_Guards_Rifle_Division
  - https://warhistory.org/2018/04/29/air-conflict-stalingrad-part-iv/
- Pin (central landing) 48.709, 44.527 [approx].

### Mamayev Kurgan, September 1942 to January 1943
- **September**: the 295th Infantry Division took it on 13 to 14 September; part was retaken on 16 September; the 95th
  (Gorishny) took the summit on 18 September and lost more than 5,000 men by 25 September.
- **From 28 September**: the 284th (Batyuk) held it.
- **The water tanks**: became German strongpoints; finally taken in January.
- **26 January**: the link-up there split the pocket (sources credit different units).
- **Zaitsev**: served in the 1047th Regiment (284th Division).
- Sources:
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mamayev_Kurgan
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/95th_Rifle_Division
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/284th_Rifle_Division
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Koltso
- Pin 48.7425, 44.5369.

### Pavlov's House, about 27 September to 25 November 1942
- **The defenders**: Sergeant Pavlov plus 3 or 4 men took it; Lieutenant Afanasyev commanded the garrison. The official
  figure is 24, more likely 25 to 30, of many nationalities (11 in Soviet counts).
- **Defences**: a PTRS on the roof, mines, wire, a communication trench. Civilians were in the cellar.
- **Duration**: 58 or 60 days (sources differ).
- **Chuikov's Paris comparison**: memoir hyperbole.
- **The trench to the mill**: [unverified].
- Sources:
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pavlov%27s_House
  - https://military-review.com/12554213-pavlovs-house-without-legends-and-myths
  - https://www.yadvashem.org/research/research-projects/soldiers/idel-khait
- Pin 48.7160, 44.5315.

### The factory district, 14 October 1942
- **37th Guards** (Zholudev): formed from the 1st Airborne Corps; crossed on 2 October with about 7,000 men; about 250
  left on 15 October.
- **The attackers**: the 14th Panzer, 305th and 389th Divisions. The tractor factory fell on 14 October.
- **Zholudev**: his command post was buried by bombs; he survived.
- **Relief**: the 138th (Lyudnikov) relieved on 16 to 17 October.
- **Operation Hubertus** (11 November) is a separate, later event.
- Sources:
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/37th_Guards_Rifle_Division
  - https://www.fireandfury.com/scenarios/redhouse.pdf
- Pins: Tractor Factory 48.795, 44.585 [approx]; Barrikady 48.775, 44.565 [approx]; Red October 48.757, 44.553
  [approx].

### Operation Uranus, 19 to 23 November 1942
- **The opening**: fog; the "Siren" bombardment at 07:20 Moscow time, Katyushas at 07:30.
- **Forces**: about 1.1 million men and 800 to 900 tanks.
- **The Kalach bridge**: taken intact by Filippov's forward detachment driving with headlights on. Sources differ on
  the date (early 22 November, or the night of 22 to 23) and the details.
- **The link-up**: about 16:00 on 23 November near Sovetsky. Green flares were not fired, and the two sides briefly
  exchanged fire.
- **Axis armour**: the Romanian 1st Armoured Division had about 100 R-2s; the 22nd Panzer had fewer than 30 to 42
  working tanks.
- **"Mice chewed the wiring"**: reportedly so; disputed.
- Sources:
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Uranus
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1st_Guards_Tank_Division
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/22nd_Panzer_Division
  - https://www.rbth.com/history/333340-how-mice-helped-red-army
- Pin (Kalach) 48.69, 43.53 [approx].

### Operation Ring, 10 January to 2 February 1943
- **The ultimatum**: rejected on 8 January.
- **The opening**: about 7,000 guns and mortars on 10 January.
- **Airfields**: Pitomnik fell on 16 January; Gumrak on 22 or 23 January.
- **The split**: the pocket was split on 26 January.
- **Paulus**: taken in the Univermag basement on 31 January.
- **The northern pocket**: ceased fire on 2 February at about 07:00.
- **Tigers**: none in the pocket.
- **Temperatures**: -20 to -30 °C [unverified].
- Sources:
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Koltso
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/502nd_Heavy_Panzer_Battalion
  - https://billdownscbs.com/2015/04/1943-von-paulus-in-custody-after.html
- Pin (Univermag) 48.708, 44.515 [approx].

## 2. D-Day campaign (June to August 1944)

General:
- **Clocks**: Double Summer Time (GMT+2) on both sides.
- **Weather on 6 June**: north-west wind force 4 to 5, waves about 1 to 1.5 m, broken cloud.
  - https://climate.copernicus.eu/new-d-day-weather-reconstruction-highlights-key-driver-military-success-and-security
- **Sunrise in Caen**: about 05:58 on 6 June and 06:19 on 18 July [unverified: the researcher's own calculation].
- **The Mk III helmet**: issued first to assault troops of the 3rd Division and the Canadians; the Mk II was common
  elsewhere.
  - https://www.iwm.org.uk/collections/item/object/30101243
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mk_III_helmet

### Pegasus Bridge, 5 to 6 June 1944
- **The force**: D Company, 2nd Ox and Bucks (Howard), 181 men in six Horsas.
- **The landings**: the first at 00:16 (pilot Wallwork); the river bridge gliders at about 00:20 to 00:21.
- **Brotheridge**: mortally wounded.
- **The defenders**: about 50 of Grenadier Regiment 736. Major Schmidt was captured.
- **The 7th Para relief**: 01:40 to 02:40 (sources differ).
- **Lovat and Millin**: arrived about 13:00 to 13:30 (sources differ). The "two and a half minutes late" anecdote is
  [unverified].
- **The demolition charges**: not placed in their chambers [ref].
- **Café Gondrée as "the first house liberated"**: folklore.
- Sources:
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Deadstick
  - https://www.pegasusarchive.org/normandy/john_howard.htm
  - https://www.pegasusarchive.org/normandy/depth_5bgd6th1.htm
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Millin
- Pin 49.2422, -0.2744.

### Merville Battery, 6 June 1944
- **The force**: 9th Parachute Battalion (Otway). About 150 of 600 to 650 men had reached the rendezvous by 02:50.
- **Equipment**: one Vickers and Bangalores; no mortars or sappers.
- **The gliders**: one reached the area and crashed about 750 yards off.
- **The guns**: 100 mm Czech howitzers (one Pegasus Archive page wrongly says 75 mm).
- **The garrison**: about 130 men under Steiner.
- **Timing**: over by about 05:00; HMS Arethusa was to fire at 05:30.
- **British losses**: 65 to 75.
- Sources:
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Merville_Gun_Battery
  - https://www.pegasusarchive.org/normandy/depth_merville.htm
- Pin 49.2694, -0.1978.

### Gold Beach, 6 June 1944
- **H-Hour**: 07:25.
- **The force**: 50th Division (Graham); 69th Brigade at King, 231st at Jig. DD tanks were landed directly on the beach.
- **The defenders**: the 352nd and 716th Divisions.
- **WN37's gun**: 75 mm or 88 mm (sources disagree); silenced by an AVRE's Petard or a Sexton (sources disagree).
- **La Rivière**: its 88 casemate was knocked out by a flail tank.
- **Hollis VC**: the only VC of D-Day.
- **47 RM Commando**: took Port-en-Bessin on 7 to 8 June.
- Sources:
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gold_Beach
  - https://www.dday-overlord.com/en/d-day/atlantic-wall/strongpoints/wn-37
  - https://www.normandywarguide.com/place/wn37-essex-yeomanry
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanley_Hollis
- Pin 49.338, -0.515 [approx].

### Sword Beach, 6 June 1944
- **The force**: 3rd Division (Rennie), 8th Brigade, 13th/18th Hussars' DD tanks.
- **The commandos**: No. 4 Commando and Kieffer's 177 French commandos cleared Ouistreham.
- **21st Panzer's counter-attack**: from about 16:00. The Staffordshire Yeomanry (with Fireflies) and anti-tank guns
  stopped it at Biéville and Périers. A battle group reached Lion-sur-Mer by about 20:00.
- **Operation Mallard's glider lift as the cause of the withdrawal**: [unverified].
- Sources:
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sword_Beach
  - https://historynet.com/die-another-day-counterattack-normandy/
  - https://www.backtonormandy.org/the-history/timeline/1944/100784-dangerous-gap-in-the-frontline.html
- Pin 49.296, -0.302 [approx].

### Hillman (WN17), 6 to 7 June 1944
- **The position**: HQ of Grenadier Regiment 736 (Krug); about 24 hectares; 18 to 20 bunkers, two command bunkers with
  armoured cupolas, about 8 Tobruks, two wire belts and minefields; no big guns.
- **The attack**: the 1st Suffolks from about 13:05; most of it taken by about 20:15.
- **The surrender**: Krug gave up at about 06:45 on 7 June with about 70 men.
- **Morris**: surrendered to the Suffolks after the bombardment.
- Sources:
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hillman_Fortress
  - https://www.amis-du-suffolk-rgt.com/?p=344
  - https://www.normandywarguide.com/place/morris-battery
- Pin 49.2653, -0.3103.

### Villers-Bocage, 13 to 14 June 1944
- **The advance**: 22nd Armoured Brigade (Hinde); 4th CLY (Cranley) into the town at about 08:30; A Squadron on to
  Point 213 with A Company, 1st Rifle Brigade.
- **The ambush**: at about 09:00 Wittmann's Tigers of heavy battalion 101 struck, destroying 13 to 14 tanks and many
  other vehicles.
- **Lockwood's Firefly**: hit Wittmann's Tiger near the square. The Tiger was disabled in the town (a 6-pdr or a
  Cromwell; sources differ).
- **The afternoon**: Panzer Lehr and Tigers attacked and several Tigers were destroyed in the streets.
- **The withdrawal**: in the evening.
- **The "Island" box on 14 June**: then withdrawn.
- **British losses**: about 217 men and 23 to 27 tanks.
- Sources:
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Villers-Bocage
  - https://www.historyofwar.org/articles/battles_villers_bocage.html
  - https://www.strijdbewijs.nl/uitbraak/bocage2aeng.htm
- Pin 49.0806, -0.6561.

### Operation Epsom and Hill 112, 26 to 30 June (and Jupiter, 10 July) 1944
- **The force**: VIII Corps (O'Connor). The attack began at 07:30 on 26 June in rain; bombers were cancelled by mist.
  The Great Storm (19 to 22 June) had delayed it.
- **Tourmauville bridge**: taken by the 2nd Argylls on 27 June.
- **Hill 112**: 11th Armoured held its north slopes from 27 to 29 June.
- **The defenders**: II SS Panzer Corps (9th and 10th SS) and the 12th SS.
- **Losses**: British about 4,000 men and about 150 tanks.
- **Operation Jupiter** (43rd Wessex) followed on 10 to 11 July.
- Sources:
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Epsom
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Jupiter_(1944)
- Pin 49.1186, -0.4594 (Wikipedia's Epsom infobox coordinate is wrong).

### Operation Charnwood, 7 to 9 July 1944
- **The raid**: 467 Lancasters and Halifaxes on the evening of 7 July. The aiming point was moved toward the city, and
  the defences were largely missed. Tonnage: 2,000 to 2,562 tons (sources differ). Civilian deaths: about 300 to 400
  (first claims were 6,000).
- **The ground attack**: at 04:20 on 8 July: the 3rd, 59th and 3rd Canadian Divisions.
- **The defenders**: the 12th SS and the 16th Luftwaffe Field Division.
- **The result**: north Caen taken by about 18:00 on 9 July.
- Sources:
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Charnwood
  - https://www.historyofwar.org/articles/operation_charnwood.html
  - https://terrycopp.com/2017/05/19/taking-caen/
- Pin 49.182, -0.370.

### Operation Goodwood, 18 to 20 July 1944
- **The bombing**: 2,077 bombers from 05:45; artillery at 06:40; H-Hour at 07:45. The ridge was reached at about 11:15.
- **The defenders**: 21st Panzer (von Luck), the 16th Luftwaffe Field Division, the 1st and 12th SS, the 503rd heavy
  battalion (Tiger I and one Tiger II company).
- **The Cagny 88s story**: rests on von Luck alone.
- **The Tiger II**: its first significant Normandy combat is given as 18 July; some sources say 11 July.
- **British tank losses**: about 250 to 500 (about 400 is the common figure).
- **Rain on 20 July**: ended it.
- Sources:
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Goodwood
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/503rd_Heavy_Panzer_Battalion
  - https://pegasusarchive.org/normandy/depth_goodwood.htm
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiger_II
- Pin (Cagny) 49.148, -0.258.

### The Falaise Pocket, 12 to 21 August 1944
- **Canadians and Poles**: Tractable from 14 August; Falaise taken on 17 August; Hill 262 (Maczuga) taken on
  19 August; Chambois linked up on 19 August; the gap sealed on 21 August. Currie VC at St-Lambert.
- **11th Armoured**: fought at Pont de Vère on 16 August and took Flers on 17 August.
- **53rd Welsh**: took Bazoches-au-Houlme on 20 August.
- **German losses**: 10,000 to 15,000 killed and 40,000 to 50,000 captured.
- **The Typhoons' effect**: on armour, small in the survey.
- Sources:
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falaise_pocket
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Hill_262
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/11th_Armoured_Division_(United_Kingdom)
  - https://www.canada.ca/en/department-national-defence/services/medals/victoria-cross-recipients/david-vivian-currie.html
- Pin (Flers) 48.749, -0.571 [approx].

## 3. Road to Berlin campaign (July 1943 to May 1945)

### Kursk, the northern shoulder, 5 to 12 July 1943
- **Soviet command**: the Central Front (Rokossovsky); the 13th Army (Pukhov); the 307th Division at Ponyri.
- **German command**: the 9th Army (Model); Ferdinands of 653 and 654 (about 90); Tigers of 505.
- **Defences**: about 504,000 anti-tank and 439,000 anti-personnel mines; up to about 2,200 anti-tank mines per km.
- **The counter-preparation**: at about 02:20 (sources say 02:00 to 02:20); the German attack at 05:30.
- **Panthers**: in the south only.
- **PTAB bomblets** first used at Kursk [ref].
- Sources:
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Kursk
  - https://balagan.info/german-order-of-battle-at-ponyri-station
  - https://warhistory.org/article/breaking-into-the-northern-kursk-salient-ii
- Pin 52.3135, 36.3026.

### Prokhorovka, 12 July 1943
- **Soviet**: the 5th Guards Tank Army (Rotmistrov); the 18th and 29th Tank Corps leading.
- **German**: the II SS Panzer Corps, with 15 serviceable Tigers on 11 July.
- **The ditch**: at Hill 252.2.
- **Tanks in the main clash**: about 900.
- **Losses** (sources disagree):
  - Soviet: 300 to 400 tanks destroyed or damaged (Zamulin 340);
  - German: 3 to 5 written off (Frieser), against 43 to 80 destroyed or damaged (Bergström);
  - the old Soviet claims are discredited.
- **12 Churchills** among the 5th Guards Tank Army's losses.
- **Weather**: thunderstorms in the afternoon.
- Sources:
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Prokhorovka
  - https://warfarehistorynetwork.com/?p=82165
  - https://defenceindepth.co/2020/01/10/in-pursuit-of-prokhorovka/
- Pin 51.0364, 36.7364.

### The Dnieper, September to November 1943
- **Bukrin**: a foothold at Zarubentsy on 22 September; the main crossings on 23 September; the airborne drop
  scattered on 24 to 25 September.
- **Lyutezh**: crossed from 26 September; Kiev freed on 6 November.
- **Hero of the Soviet Union awards**: 2,438.
- **Crossing methods**: improvised rafts.
- Sources:
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_the_Dnieper
  - https://warfarehistorynetwork.com/?p=14436
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Kiev_(1943)
- Pin 49.9472, 31.3158.

### Bagration (Bobruisk), 22 June to August 1944
- **Start date**: the main attacks on 23 to 24 June (sources give 22 or 23 June).
- **Bobruisk**: the pocket closed about 27 June; the town fell on 29 June. 65th and 3rd Armies; the 1st Guards Tank
  Corps through the marsh on corduroy roads.
- **Minsk**: freed on 3 July (one line in English Wikipedia says 4 July).
- **German losses**: 300,000 to 400,000 (Zaloga, Frieser); the Soviet claim is higher.
- Sources:
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bobruysk_offensive
  - https://eng.belarusenc.by/topics/worldwarii/9414/
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Bagration
- Pin 53.14, 29.22 [approx].

### Vistula–Oder and Lisów, 12 to 13 January 1945
- **Start**: Konev on 12 January (brought forward for the weather); Zhukov on 14 January; Warsaw on 17 January.
- **Lisów, 13 January**: heavy battalion 424 (ex-501; there was no "524" in this fight) with about 23 King Tigers and
  29 Tiger Is, plus the 16th Panzer's Panthers, against the 61st Guards Tank Brigade.
  - Both commanders were killed.
  - The German claim of IS-2s there is disputed (Isaev says none).
  - One line dates the fight to 12 January.
- **The first King Tiger action in the East**: Oględów, 11 to 13 August 1944 (s.Pz.Abt 501; Oskin's T-34-85).
- Sources:
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vistula–Oder_offensive
  - https://www.tankarchives.com/2015/02/cheating-at-statistics-10-liars-at-lisow.html
  - https://cyclowiki.org/wiki/Бой_у_деревни_Лисув
  - https://www.tankarchives.com/2013/07/king-tigers-at-ogledow.html
- Pin 50.6911, 20.6661.

### The Oder bridgehead, Kienitz, from 31 January 1945
- **The crossing**: the 5th Shock Army's advance units crossed on 31 January and found no garrison.
- **The bridgehead**: about 4 km by 2 km; fought over for 76 days.
- **Weather**: a thaw followed the blizzard.
- **Küstrin**: fell on 29 to 31 March.
- **The leave-train story**: [unverified].
- Sources:
  - https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kienitz
  - https://europeremembers.com/pois/2574/memorials-at-kienitz
  - https://warfarehistorynetwork.com/?p=37837
- Pin 52.6742, 14.4411.

### The Seelow Heights, 16 to 19 April 1945
- **The opening**: a 25-minute barrage at 05:00 Moscow time (03:00 Berlin time); about 140 searchlights at 05:25 (the
  popular figure is 143); the attack at 05:30.
- **The lights**: diffused by the dust.
- **Heinrici**: pulled back from the first line.
- **German counter-attacks on 18 April**: including heavy battalion 503's King Tigers.
- **Soviet killed**: about 5,000 to more than 30,000 (sources disagree).
- **Fog**: [unverified].
- Sources:
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_the_Seelow_Heights
  - https://www.tankarchives.com/2014/10/seelow-spotlights.html
- Pin 52.5298, 14.4261.

### Berlin, 21 to 28 April 1945
- **Weidling**: took command on 23 April.
- **The Teltow Canal**: forced on 24 April.
- **The ring**: closed on 24 to 25 April.
- **Tempelhof**: on 26 April.
- **The defenders**: about 45,000 troops plus about 40,000 Volkssturm, police and Hitler Youth.
- **The exact Spree crossing points**: [unverified].
- Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Berlin
- Pin (Tempelhof) 52.473, 13.402 [approx].

### The Moltke Bridge, 28 to 29 April 1945
- **The bridge**: blown but sagging; barricades about 1 m thick.
- **The crossing**: at dusk on 28 April.
- **The Interior Ministry**: from 07:00 on 29 April; secured at about 04:30 on 30 April.
- Sources:
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moltke_Bridge
  - https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/Штурм_Рейхстага
- Pin 52.5216, 13.3707 [approx].

### The Reichstag, 30 April to 2 May 1945
- **The approach**: a flooded U-Bahn trench across the Königsplatz.
- **The assault**: after 30 minutes of direct fire from 89 guns, at 13:00.
- **The 14:25 flag report**: premature.
- **Flags**: Makov's group at about 22:30 and Sorokin's at about 22:40; Berest, Yegorov and Kantaria with Victory
  Banner No. 5 at about 22:00 Berlin time; it was moved to the dome on 2 May. About 40 flags in all. Russia's Institute
  of Military History calls the question of who was first unresolved.
- **Surrender**: the garrison early on 2 May; Weidling at about 06:00.
- **Khaldei's photograph**: staged on 2 May.
- **The Victory Banner**: 188 by 82 cm; one of nine assault flags.
- Sources:
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raising_a_Flag_over_the_Reichstag
  - https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/Знамя_Победы
  - https://crwflags.com/FOTW/FLAGS/su^vctry.html
- Pin 52.5186, 13.3762.

## 4. African campaign (August 1940 to October 1942)

Main sources: the South African official history (Orpen, "East African and Abyssinian Campaigns",
https://www.ibiblio.org/hyperwar/UN/SouthAfrica/EAfrica/index.html), Wikipedia, samilitaryhistory.org, the London
Gazette despatches, IWM, and Belgian sources.

### Tug Argan, 11 to 15 August 1940
- **British force** (Godwin-Austen): 1st Northern Rhodesia Regiment, 2nd (Nyasaland) KAR, 1st East African Light
  Battery (3.7-inch; two guns lost on 12 August), Punjabi battalions, Somaliland Camel Corps; the 2nd Black Watch in
  reserve.
- **Italian force**: De Simone's central column, with 11 colonial and 3 Blackshirt battalions, part of a company of
  M11/39s, L3/35s, armoured cars and 21 batteries.
- **The hills**: Mill Hill fell on 12 August and Observation Hill on 15 August.
- **Wilson VC**: awarded as killed, but he had been captured.
- **British losses**: 38 killed. Italian figures conflict.
- **The coordinate**: the Wikipedia coordinate is Hargeisa, not the gap.
- Sources:
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Tug_Argan
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_conquest_of_British_Somaliland
  - https://www.thegazette.co.uk/London/issue/37594/page/2722/data.pdf
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_Charles_Twelves_Wilson
- Pin 9.95, 44.6 [approx].

### El Wak, 16 December 1940 (alternate)
- **The raid**: Gold Coast Regiment (Dickforce), Pienaar's South Africans, a KAR company; light tanks could not pass the
  wire; Ballenden's Bangalore torpedo.
- **The division's name**: sources disagree (12th African or 2nd (African) Division).
- Sources:
  - https://www.ibiblio.org/hyperwar/UN/SouthAfrica/EAfrica/EAfrica-6.html
  - https://samilitaryhistory.org/lectures/elwak.html
  - https://ditsong.org.za/en/el-wak-16-december-1940/

### The Juba River, 17 to 22 February 1941
- **Crossings**: the 1st SA Brigade at Yonte on 17 February (the "Union Bridge" by 19 February); the Gold Coast Brigade
  at Mabungo; the 22nd East African Brigade cut the Jelib road.
- **Mogadishu**: the Nigerians entered on 25 February.
- **Italian losses**: about 31,000 put out of action.
- **The river**: about 580 ft wide near Gobwen.
- **The Bulo Erillo action**: [unverified].
- Sources:
  - https://www.ibiblio.org/hyperwar/UN/SouthAfrica/EAfrica/EAfrica-15.html
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_African_campaign_(World_War_II)
- Pin (Jelib) 0.49, 42.78 [approx].

### Keren, February to 27 March 1941
- **The main attackers**: the 4th and 5th Indian Divisions (Platt).
- **The Brigade d'Orient** (Monclar): BM3, raised in Chad (Garbay), took Fort Kub-Kub on 21 to 23 February (the first
  Free French victory; the days vary by source), then fought at Engiahat on 14 to 28 March.
- **The Italian defenders** (Carnimeo; Lorenzini killed): Granatieri di Savoia, Alpini, Bersaglieri, colonial brigades.
- **Losses**: British 536 killed. Italian figures vary widely.
- Sources:
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Keren
  - https://www.ordredelaliberation.fr/en/army
  - https://theatrum-belli.com/in-memoriam-idriss-doursan-compagnon-de-la-liberation-decede-le-10-juillet-1965/?pdf=367566
- Pin 15.777, 38.451.

### Gideon Force and the patriots in Gojjam, January to May 1941
- **The Emperor's return**: crossed in January; Bure on 14 March.
- **Debre Markos**: taken on 4 or 6 April (sources differ).
- **Addis Ababa**: entered on 5 May.
- **Gideon Force**: Wingate; the Frontier Battalion (Boustead); the 2nd Ethiopian Battalion; about 800 plus 800 men;
  no artillery; the camels died.
- **Maraventano's surrender**: 19 or 23 May (sources differ).
- **Error to avoid**: the Dembecha article's "1942" is a typo for 1941.
- Sources:
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gideon_Force
  - https://www.britishmilitaryhistory.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/sites/124/2020/01/Chronology-of-Events.pdf
  - https://muse.jhu.edu/journals/northeast_african_studies/v010/10.3.marcus.pdf
- Pin (Debre Markos) 10.33, 37.72 [approx].

### Marda Pass and Harar, March 1941 (alternate)
- **The attack**: the 23rd Nigerian Brigade attacked at midday on 21 March; the pass was empty on 22 March.
- **Harar**: entered on 25 or 26 March.
- Source: https://www.ibiblio.org/hyperwar/UN/SouthAfrica/EAfrica/EAfrica-17.html

### Mega, 15 to 18 February 1941 (alternate)
- **The weather**: rain, mist and mud (hail is not in the official history). The Italians feared a phantom "4th
  Australian Division".
- Sources:
  - https://www.ibiblio.org/hyperwar/UN/SouthAfrica/EAfrica/EAfrica-13.html
  - https://scientiamilitaria.journals.ac.za/pub/article/view/356

### The Lakes: Wadara and Colito, May 1941
- **Wadara**: the 24th Gold Coast Brigade (22 Europeans and 96 Africans killed).
- **Colito**: the 22nd East African Brigade forced the Billate on 19 May; Italian tank counter-attacks; Leakey VC.
- **Captures**: Colito 489 Italians and 300 colonial troops; Soddu about 4,800, with 6 medium and 4 light tanks.
- **The Italian medium tanks' type**: [unverified]. The 321st company's M11/39s were last in action at Agordat in
  January 1941.
- Sources:
  - https://www.ibiblio.org/hyperwar/UN/SouthAfrica/EAfrica/EAfrica-21.html
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigel_Gray_Leakey
  - https://tanks-encyclopedia.com/ww2/italy/321a-and-322a-compagnie-carri-m-in-the-east-italian-africa-campaign/
- Pin 7.31, 38.09 [approx].

### Saïo, March to July 1941
- **Gambela**: taken on 25 March (Belgian-Congolese troops with two KAR companies).
- **Earlier attacks**: the Bortai on 15 and 21 April; Mogi on 9 June.
- **The final assault**: the crossing forced on 2 July and the flank attack along a goat path on 3 July.
- **The surrender**: on 6 or 7 July (sources differ): Gazzera, 8 more generals and 6,454 men.
- **Belgo-Congolese dead**: 462, about 80% from disease.
- **The force's size**: 8,000 in one source; 5,700 in another.
- **De Simone commanding early**: dubious.
- **Rifles**: Mauser types [unverified].
- Sources:
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Saïo
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Force_Publique
  - https://www.egyptstudycircle.org.uk/ESCmembers/Military/WWII/Belgum/BCongo.pdf
- Pin 8.53, 34.80.

### Gondar and Kulkaber, November 1941
- **The attack**: the 12th African Division (Fowkes); the 25th and 26th EA Brigades; the 1st EA Pioneers; the Kenya
  Armoured Car Regiment; patriots (Ringrose).
- **Kulkaber**: Ugolini's Carabinieri; the first attack failed on 13 November; the pass fell on 21 November (Italian
  losses 1,003 killed).
- **Gondar**: the assault at 05:30 on 27 November; Nasi surrendered.
- **Mottet's CR.42 sortie**: 20 or 22 November (sources differ).
- **Prisoners**: about 10,000 Italian and 12,000 African troops.
- Sources:
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Gondar
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Culqualber
  - https://www.thegazette.co.uk/London/issue/37655/supplement/3712/data.pdf
  - https://www.carabinieri.it/docs/default-source/evento/2014/approfondimento-della-battaglia-di-culqualber.pdf
- Pins: Gondar 12.60, 37.47; Kulkaber 12.2, 37.9 [approx].

### Sidi Rezegh, 23 November 1941
- **The brigade**: the 5th SA Infantry Brigade (Armstrong, captured): 3rd Transvaal Scottish, South African Irish
  (1st or 2nd; sources differ), 2nd Regiment Botha; the 3rd Field Regiment's 25-pdrs.
- **The attackers**: the 15th Panzer and Ariete (and the 21st Panzer in some accounts).
- **Trenches**: about 23 cm deep in the limestone.
- **Strength**: about 5,800 in, under 2,000 out.
- **Job Maseko**: captured at Tobruk; awarded the Military Medal.
- Sources:
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/5th_Infantry_Brigade_(South_Africa)
  - https://www.samilitaryhistory.org/vol106dm.html
  - https://samilhistory.com/2016/11/23/sidi-rezegh-south-african-blood-helps-turn-the-tide-in-north-africa/
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Job_Maseko
- Pin 31.98, 24.1 [approx].

### Madagascar, Ironclad and Stream Line Jane, 1942
- **Ironclad** (Diego Suarez, 5 to 7 May): British; rejected as a mission.
- **Stream Line Jane**:
  - the 22nd EA Brigade (Dimoline) landed at Majunga on 10 September;
  - Tananarive fell on 23 or 29 September (sources differ);
  - the 7th SA Brigade's armoured cars;
  - Andriamanalina on 18 October (a KAR victory, about 800 surrendered; the location is [unverified]);
  - the armistice at Ambalavao on 6 November (5 November in some sources); Annet surrendered on 8 November.
- **Vichy forces**: about 8,000, of them about 6,000 Malagasy and many Senegalese; 17 MS.406 and 10 Potez 63; "6 tanks"
  of unknown type. The Foreign Legion's presence is doubtful.
- Sources:
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Madagascar
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/22nd_(East_Africa)_Infantry_Brigade
  - https://www.samilitaryhistory.org/vol092jc.html
  - https://www.britishmilitaryhistory.co.uk/docs-e-w-s-africa-1930-1947-madagascar-1942
  - https://warfarehistorynetwork.com/?p=22968
- Pin 20.0, 47.1 [approx].

### El Alamein, 23 October 1942
- **Lightfoot**: began at 21:40.
- **The 1st SA Division** (Pienaar): the 2nd and 3rd Brigades on Miteiriya Ridge; the Natal Mounted Rifles on their
  objective at 23:50; the Cape Town Highlanders on the ridge by 08:00; about 600 SA casualties by 26 October.
- **The defenders**: the 164th Light, Trento, Bologna and the 7th Bersaglieri.
- **The Devil's Gardens**: about 500,000 mines, up to 8 km deep.
- **The full-moon timing**: [ref].
- **Shermans**: first combat at Second Alamein [ref].
- Sources:
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1st_South_African_Infantry_Division
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Battle_of_El_Alamein_order_of_battle
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devil%27s_gardens
- Pin 30.83, 28.95 [approx].

## 7. Notes on sensitive content

- **SS formations** (Villers-Bocage, Epsom, Prokhorovka, Berlin): named only in this file and, as unit names, in a few
  briefings' history notes. In battle they are plain German with no SS insignia, as the owner's rules require.
- **Blackshirt battalions** in East Africa: present historically, not depicted as such.
- **Hitler Youth** in Berlin: present historically, not depicted; the Volkssturm appear as older men.
- **Atrocities**: the Abbaye d'Ardenne murders of Canadian prisoners (June 1944) and the many crimes of the war on the
  Eastern Front are real and are not used as game content or briefing colour.
- **The South African flag of the time**: see `NATIONS.md` section 1; the default is the Union Flag.
- **Colonial context**: the African campaign's African soldiers served in colonial armies, and many were not
  volunteers in the modern sense. The briefings speak in a period commander's voice but must not demean them; the
  epilogue (Job Maseko) and the victory screens honour them by name of unit.
